Amid the growing textile waste crisis, we developed an educational board game to raise awareness and empower individuals to see themselves as active agents of change. Through a shared experience of design and play, the project cultivates imagination, critical reflection, and a sense of collective responsibility.

I led the development of Aftermath, a board game themed around textile waste, by **designing and facilitating 14 design thinking workshops**. Each session brought together at least six participants to collaboratively explore sustainable systems and actions through game design. My role included **presenting research** on board games and game-based learning, **developing** inclusive and engaging **workshop activities**, **prototyping** both on paper and in Adobe Creative Suite, conducting multiple rounds of **user testing** to iterate and refine the game, and **documenting participants' reflection** at the end of each workshop.

### Game Evolution: From Competitive Play to Collective Action

The initial version of the game was designed as a competitive experience involving three roles: **Consumer, Advocate, and Landfill Manager**. Consumers advanced on the board using action cards, while Advocates and the Landfill Manager influenced gameplay through policy and disaster cards. The win conditions were asymmetrical—Consumers and Advocates aimed to avoid the landfill, while the Landfill Manager's goal was to direct at least one player there. This setup introduced the basic dynamics of environmental decision-making but framed them in opposition.

In the second version, the game expanded to include more relatable and socially grounded roles, such as **Teacher, Lawyer, and Fast-Fashion Company Leader**. The Advocate and Landfill Manager were removed as player roles and reimagined as interactive elements on the board. When players landed on specific grids, they could draw a policy or disaster card, maintaining the influence of those roles while ensuring that every player remained actively engaged. This version also refined the game's objectives and introduced more structured movement and action paths for each character.

The third and current version transformed the game into a **cooperative experience**, better reflecting the collaborative nature of real-world environmental efforts. Each player now takes on a unique societal role (Teacher, Lawyer, Policy Maker, or Fast-Fashion Company Leader) with an individual pathway to impact change. **Skill cards** allow players to support one another’s progress, while **Non-Profit grids** highlight real-world initiatives working to reduce textile waste. The game’s win condition was unified: either all players succeed together by reaching the “Closet” and mitigating textile waste, or all lose. This cooperative structure promotes empathy, shared responsibility, and systems thinking.

### How to Play
Aftermath Board Game is a multiplayer physical board game where each player is assigned a persona to play as: Teacher, Lawyer, Fast Fashion Company Leader, and Policy Maker. Each character **starts on the start square in the middle of the board**. One by one, players **draw action cards** from the center pick pile that dictate movement toward a "closet" for positive consumption behaviors or a "landfill" for negative ones. The ultimate **goal** is for **all characters to reach the closet**, as the game is lost if any character lands in the landfill. There are five total card piles, **one general pick pile in the center of the board and four unique persona card piles** that only the player of the matching persona can pick from.  Players start the game picking from the general pile, but if a player draws a "pick your card" card, the player then draws a **specialized card from their character's unique pile**. Each player’s persona includes a **superpower** they can use once to save themselves or another player from a bad fate. Superpowers are as followers: **Teacher** can send a person to “START”, **Lawyer** can exempt a person from Disaster Card, **Policy Maker** allows a person roll the dice and move forward double the dice amount and **Fast-Fashion Company Leader** can save a person from landfill and roll the dice to decide how many steps to move forward. On top of personal powers, there are nonprofit cards. Players that encounter **"nonprofit"** cards give them an **advantage** when moving toward the closet. **"Disaster"** cards cause players to **move toward the landfill**.
