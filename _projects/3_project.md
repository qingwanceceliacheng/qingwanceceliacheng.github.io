In this Human-Computer Interaction Class project, our goal was to improve communication between college professors and students. While academic communication happens in many forms—such as in-class interactions, emails, announcements, and grading—we chose to focus specifically on course evaluations, a critical yet often underutilized channel for feedback.

After initial research and interview, our central design question became:
**How might we encourage honest, constructive feedback in course evaluations?**

To address this, we developed a **customizable survey platform** that incorporates a **tagging system**. The tagging feature helps guide students to provide specific, actionable responses, especially when they are dissatisfied. We created a comprehensive set of tags for each question while also allowing both students and professors to add their own, making the feedback process more personalized and adaptable.

The platform features a clear and intuitive interface. We designed a survey outline page that uses breadcrumb navigation and a side menu, so users always know where they are—for example, whether they’re viewing Live Surveys or Past Surveys. Within a survey, sections are clearly labeled (e.g., Course Evaluation, Instructor Evaluation, Materials Evaluation, Submit), helping students navigate the process easily.

We implemented several UX features to support clarity and usability:
- **Error messages** to prevent accidental deletions, submissions, or edits
- **Tooltip popups** (small “?” buttons) that explain unfamiliar features
- **Survey templates** to speed up creation
- **Submission feedback** for both professors and students
- **Color-coded responses**, with green for positive and red for negative feedback
- **Content moderation** tools to block disrespectful or inappropriate comments
- **Visual distinction** between professor and student views, using blue and green themes respectively

Through this project, we aimed to create a respectful and user-friendly environment where students feel safe to share genuine feedback, and professors receive clear insights that can improve teaching and learning experiences.
